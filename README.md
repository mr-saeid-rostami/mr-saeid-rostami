### /// THE TRAJECTORY: AN UNVARNISHED TRUTH
I did not arrive at Computer Science through a conventional or effortless corridor. 

Growing up, I was driven by a fundamental question: *Can I build a machine that thinks and computes on my own terms?* Misguided by conventional societal pressures, I initially pursued natural sciences (Biology), seeking financial independence to fund my engineering curiosity. Later, I spent three years studying Psychology. Yet, inside those disciplines, I felt intellectually constrained. The systems were fascinating—from molecular interactions to cognitive patterns—but they lacked the structural freedom of computation.

In **2023**, with just one year left before earning a degree in Psychology, I chose radical honesty over comfort. I walked away from a safe exit and rebuilt my trajectory from ground zero by enrolling in Computer Science. 

To transform oneself so late in the game is like converting a bookstore into a restaurant, and then transforming that restaurant into an automotive facility. It carries risk, friction, and failure. But failure is simply raw data for someone who refuses to stagnate.

### /// ON NUMBERS, METRICS, AND RIGOR
I do not offer a spotless historical academic record, nor have my test scores always reflected conventional excellence. I do not hide behind metrics. 

Where my transcripts might show variance, my reality shows persistence. I view computing not as an algorithmic rat race for grades, but as a window into the elegance of existence. Computation is where human cognition, physical logic, and architecture intersect. My commitment is not measured by standardized ease, but by my capacity to sit with complex, uncomfortable problems until they yield.
